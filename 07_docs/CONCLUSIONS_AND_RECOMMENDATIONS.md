# Conclusions and Recommendations

## Conclusion

The Kenya Food Security Project combined maize production, retail price, climate, and food-security data. The central pattern is that maize production alone did not correspond to improving national food-security outcomes. The prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity increased from 50.7% in the 2014-2016 rolling period to 72.8% in the 2021-2023 rolling period, even though maize production remained substantial and rose above 4 million tonnes in 2023.

Climate variability likely affected the stability of production through changes in rainfall, heat, drought conditions, yield, and area harvested. Market prices add the access dimension: a household can live in a food-producing area yet be unable to afford food when prices rise or income is unstable. The analysis therefore suggests that food security depends on availability, affordability, access, and stability together.

This is an exploratory national-level analysis. The food-security measure is a three-year average and is available only for 2015-2022 in the project dataset. The evidence shows association and context, not proof that one indicator caused the observed food-security trend.

## Recommendations

1. Pair production support with affordability measures, such as targeted cash assistance or school-feeding programmes during high-price and drought periods.
2. Invest in climate-resilient agriculture: drought-tolerant crops, extension support, water management, soil-health practices, and climate advisories.
3. Reduce post-harvest losses and improve storage, so harvests are available for longer and farmers are not forced to sell immediately after production.
4. Improve farmer market access and price information, while monitoring consumer maize prices for sudden increases.
5. Add county-level food-security, poverty, and household-consumption data in a future project phase to identify the communities most at risk.

## Portfolio statement

"Kenya's maize production was important but insufficient to ensure food security. Food insecurity rose over the available food-security period, showing why production must be analysed alongside climate risk, market prices, affordability, and household access to food."
